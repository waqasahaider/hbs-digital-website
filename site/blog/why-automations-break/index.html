Why most business automations quietly break — and how to keep them alive

The dirty secret of automation is that building it is the easy part. Keeping it working — as tools, prices and models change underneath it — is what separates an asset from a liability.

Software doesn't stand still

The apps your automation connects to update constantly. An API changes, a login expires, a form field is renamed, a pricing tier shifts. Any one of these can silently stop a workflow — and because it fails quietly, you often find out only when a customer does.

Most “failed AI projects” didn't fail at launch. They worked, then broke months later because nobody was watching them.

Models improve — if you keep up

The AI models behind these systems get cheaper and more capable every few months. An automation left untouched is running on last year's model at last year's cost. Regular tuning keeps it accurate and keeps your usage bills sensible.

What good care looks like

  • Monitoring: automated checks that catch a break before your customers do.
  • Break-fix: a fast response when a connected tool changes.
  • Improvement: monthly tuning of prompts and models as they evolve.
  • Reporting: a plain summary of what the system actually did and saved.

The part most agencies skip

Plenty of vendors will build you something impressive and disappear. The ongoing care — the unglamorous monitoring and fixing — is what keeps automation delivering a year later. It's why we treat it as a first-class service, not an afterthought.

Build it once; keep it alive on purpose. That's the difference between an automation that pays back for years and one that becomes another abandoned tool.
